## Predictions

- P-R154-1: **PASS** — all three models solve; all witnesses
  satisfiable.
- P-R154-2: **PASS** — all three structural asserts that should
  fail do, each mirroring its slice's temporal defect; the two
  asserts that should hold, hold.
- P-R154-3: **PASS, with a named friction** — models are 80–103
  lines, but each took several solver iterations, all on Alloy
  idiom, none on the slice semantics: primed variable names
  rejected in predicate parameters, no ternary expressions, Int
  scope syntax, and — the structural one — ordered-trace scopes
  are exact, so the State scope must equal the trace length
  (7/8/9 states here; a scope off by one makes the witness
  silently unsatisfiable and asserts vacuously true). A scaling
  phase must generate or search scopes, not hand-tune them.
- P-R154-4: **PASS** — no Alloy result contradicts its TLA+
  verdict; where both checkers speak (limit, double-hold,
  tiling, balance), they agree.

## Assessment

Feasibility: yes. A confirmed slice's structural shadow model is
an hour-scale authoring task with today's engine, and Alloy's
small-scope counterexamples are snapshots a reader can inspect
without replaying a trace. Added value over TLA alone, honestly:
for these three slices, none yet in findings — Alloy agreed
everywhere. Its plausible value is in slices whose interesting
property is relational (disjointness, tiling, holder/count
consistency) rather than interleaving-shaped, and as an
independent second checker whose disagreement would itself be a
finding (P-R154-4's stop rule). Recommended next step for the
owner: commission the skeleton generator only if structural
properties become part of curated gating; otherwise keep Alloy
as an on-demand adjudication shadow for confirmed slices, with
the exact-scope friction noted for whoever authors them.
